# Costs Lawyers: what the CLSB's own documents say

Read 24 November 2026 from the CLSB Costs Lawyer Handbook
(<https://clsb.info/for-costs-lawyers/costs-lawyer-handbook/>). The documents are PDFs;
text was extracted locally from the copies fetched that day. Versions as printed on each.

**Not read:** the CLSB's guidance "Using AI in Your Work" (referred to in the supervision
note); the Price Transparency, Conflicts of Interest, Economic Crime and Social Media
notes; the Ethics Hub; the SRA comparison is against `sra/` files in this folder, not
re-read today.

## Who is regulated: individuals only

- Guidance for costs law firms (23 April 2026, v4): "Because the CLSB only regulates
  individuals and not organisations, these too are "unregulated" organisations even if
  they are owned by or employ regulated individuals."
- Same note: "Costs law firms often employ a combination of regulated Costs Lawyers,
  unregulated costs advisors, paralegals and other staff."
- Same note: "if as an unregulated costs law firm you do hold money that belongs to your
  clients, the relevant account should not be in a Costs Lawyer's name."
- Practising certificates page (12 October 2026): "Your Costs Lawyer number is your
  unique regulatory identifier. It will start with the letters "CL" and will be followed
  by a series of numbers."

## Practising Rules (1 August 2020, v4)

- Rule 1: "No person shall be entitled to practise as a Costs Lawyer unless: (a) they
  have qualified as a Costs Lawyer in accordance with the Training Rules; (b) they have a
  current Practising Certificate ...; (c) they comply with CPD requirements ...; and (d)
  they have professional indemnity insurance in accordance with Rule 9."
- Rule 9.1: "practise with the benefit of professional indemnity insurance of a minimum
  £100,000 (any one claim) to include loss of documents", and assess risk so that cover is
  "in excess of the minimum".

## Code of Conduct (8 April 2024)

Seven principles: 1 honesty, integrity and independence; 2 duty to the court; 3 best
interests of the client; 4 good quality of work and service; 5 openness with regulators
and the Legal Ombudsman; 6 fairness and respect; 7 confidentiality.

- Definitions. Client: "The person for whom a Costs Lawyer acts". Professional client:
  "Any person or organisation authorised to carry out reserved legal activities under the
  LSA, or any unauthorised costs advisor, that instructs a Costs Lawyer to provide
  services to or in relation to a client".
- 1.3: "You must not give false or misleading information to anyone with whom you deal."
- 1.4: must not be "misleading or inaccurate about the nature or scope of the services
  you are offering, who will be legally responsible for undertaking them, the extent to
  which they are covered by regulation and insurance".
- 2.2: "You must not knowingly or recklessly either mislead the court, attempt to mislead
  the court, or allow the court to be misled."
- 3.1: "You must act in the best interests of your client regardless of the consequences
  for your professional client or other intermediary."
- 3.2 and 3.3: an effective complaints procedure; complaints dealt with "within a maximum
  eight week period".
- 3.4: new clients told in writing of fees or charging structure, the right to complain,
  how, and the Legal Ombudsman where it applies.
- 3.6: "You must not accept client money save for disbursements and payment of your
  proper professional fees."
- 3.8: maintain insurance that complies with the Practising Rules.
- 4.5: "keep accurate records of that work".
- 7.1: "You must keep the affairs of your client confidential unless disclosure is
  required or allowed by law or if the client consents in writing to disclosure".

## Files and data

Retention of client data and files (12 December 2024, v3):

- "If you are obtaining a client's personal data for the purpose of providing them with
  legal advice or other services, you will also be a data controller".
- "it is common to retain client files for six years after the end of the matter, as this
  is the usual limitation period for breach of contract and negligence claims."
- A worked scenario is a bill of costs prepared on the instruction of a professional
  client, with the "ultimate client" asking for the file.

## Supervision, outsourcing and AI

Delegation and supervision by Costs Lawyers (2 November 2026, v1):

- "Because you remain responsible for regulatory compliance and client outcomes when
  tasks are delegated, you must retain proper oversight of the matter and supervise your
  colleague appropriately."
- "Where any part of the work is outsourced to a third party, your contractual
  responsibilities to the client mean that you will need to ensure that any of the
  outsourced work which is delegated is effectively supervised."
- Para 56: "ensuring that outputs produced with the assistance of AI are subject to
  appropriate human review, scrutiny and professional judgement."
- Para 57: "must ensure that an authorised individual retains ultimate responsibility for
  any legal services delivered with AI assistance."

Client care letters (22 October 2026, v9): "You should also advise the client of the
scope and relevant terms of any proposed outsourcing arrangements (if applicable)."

## Reserved work and unregulated draftsmen

- Code: a Costs Lawyer may exercise a right of audience, conduct litigation and administer
  oaths, in matters that relate to costs.
- CLSB FAQs: "Unregulated advisers can't carry out reserved legal activities, unless they
  benefit from one of the limited exemptions that are set out in legislation."
- CLSB FAQs: "A person does not need to be qualified and regulated before they can advise
  consumers about matters relating to legal costs."

## How this differs from an SRA firm (Claude's reading)

| | SRA firm | Costs Lawyer |
|---|---|---|
| What is regulated | the firm and its solicitors | the individual only; a costs firm is unregulated |
| Identifier | firm SRA number | personal "CL" number |
| Minimum insurance | not re-read today | £100,000 any one claim |
| Client money | holds it under the Accounts Rules | none, except fees and disbursements |
| Whose file | the firm's own client | usually an instructing firm's client: a professional client and an ultimate client |
| Records | see the SRA files in `../automation-portal/.claude/context/sra/` | "common" to keep six years after the matter ends; guidance, not a rule |
| AI | SRA warning notice | human review; an authorised individual keeps responsibility |

An unregulated costs draftsman has none of the above: no regulator, no identifier, no
required insurance, no Legal Ombudsman route. Anything the site says about answering a
regulator's rules does not apply to them.
