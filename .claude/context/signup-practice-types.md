# Kinds of practice that might sign up, and what differs between them

Read on 24 November 2026 for the sign-up form. Quotes are from the pages named. Where a
page did not say, that is recorded: nothing below is filled in from memory.

## What the SRA authorises

Source: SRA guidance "Firm authorisation", updated 15 October 2025,
<https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/guidance/firm-authorisation/>.

- Three kinds of authorised firm: a **recognised sole practice**, a **recognised body**
  (a legal services body) and a **licensed body** (an alternative business structure).
- "A sole practice is where a solicitor practises on their own account, providing
  services in their own name, or under a trading name".
- A legal services body is "a partnership, company or limited liability partnership
  (LLP)".
- "If you are practising through a limited company, that is not a sole practice and the
  company itself must be authorised."
- Not stated on the pages read: that every authorised firm has an SRA number.

## Freelance solicitors

Sources: SRA "Preparing to become a sole practitioner or an SRA-regulated independent
solicitor" (updated 25 November 2019); SRA "Freelancer notification and updates"
(updated 20 July 2026); SRA Authorisation of Individuals Regulations, regulation 10.2.
**The regulation's sub-paragraphs came through a summarising reader, not verbatim: check
them against the live page before relying on the wording.**

- "a self-employed solicitor who is: practising on their own, and does not employ anyone
  else in connection with the services they provide".
- "a freelance solicitor is authorised by us as an individual", in contrast to a
  recognised sole practice, "a type of firm authorised by us".
- For reserved work, regulation 10.2(b) as reported: three years' practice since
  admission; "self-employed and practise in your own name, and not through a trading name
  or service company"; "do not employ anyone in connection with the services that you
  provide"; "engaged directly by the client with your fees payable directly to you"; a
  practising address in the UK; "adequate and appropriate" indemnity insurance; no client
  money except disbursement money under Accounts Rule 2.1(d).
- "you cannot be practising in a partnership, as a consultant on behalf of someone else,
  or through a limited company."

## Costs Lawyers and costs draftsmen

Source: Costs Lawyer Standards Board FAQs, <https://clsb.info/for-the-public/faqs/>.

- "A person does not need to be qualified and regulated before they can advise consumers"
  about legal costs. "Unregulated costs advisers are sometimes called costs draftsmen, or
  similar terms."
- "The names of all Costs Lawyers regulated by the Costs Lawyer Standards Board are shown
  on our Register of Costs Lawyers." No individual number is mentioned.
- "Costs Lawyers must not accept a client's money, unless this is in payment of the Costs
  Lawyer's fees". A Costs Lawyer in an SRA-regulated firm "can accept your money 'on
  account'".
- Not stated: that a practising certificate is mandatory (the page refers to those "who
  have a current practising certificate").

## Who can hold a legal aid contract

Source: Legal Aid Agency, 2024 Standard Civil Contract Application Guide V4.0, section 5
(PDF read from a saved copy).

- 5.1: "This procurement process is open to any organisation able to meet the
  requirements set out in this Application Guide and the 2024 Standard Civil Contract."
- 5.2: "The LAA will only contract with single legal entities (including individuals who
  are sole traders)."
- 5.5: "Applicants must be able to demonstrate that they hold all necessary
  authorisations and licences to conduct Contract Work."
- Not read: sections 7 to 12 (the detailed requirements, including quality standards and
  supervision). So whether a freelance solicitor can in practice hold a contract is **not
  settled**; a costs draftsman does not do Contract Work and would not hold one.

## Business registration and tax

- LLP: "register the LLP with Companies House" (gov.uk, "Set up and run a limited
  liability partnership", updated 2 September 2026). LLPs need "2 or more members".
- General partnership: the gov.uk steps are to choose a name, choose a nominated partner
  and "register with HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC)"
  (<https://www.gov.uk/set-up-business-partnership>). No Companies House step is listed;
  the page does not say in terms that there is none.
- VAT: "You need to register for VAT if you go over the registration threshold (or expect
  to)"; the threshold is taxable turnover "More than £90,000"
  (<https://www.gov.uk/how-vat-works/vat-thresholds>). So any kind of practice may or may
  not be registered.
- Data protection fee: "organisations (including sole traders) that use personal
  information need to pay a data protection fee, unless they are exempt"
  (<https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/>). The exemptions were not
  read.

## What follows for the form (Claude's reading, not a source)

- The first question is two things, not one: **what you are regulated as** (an SRA firm,
  a freelance solicitor, a Costs Lawyer, an unregulated costs draftsman) and, for firms
  and costs practices, **the legal form** (sole, partnership, LLP, company).
- A freelance solicitor has no trading name, no company number, no staff to invite and
  gives their own SRA number, not a firm's.
- Costs Lawyers and draftsmen are not the firm whose clients' files they hold. They act
  for instructing solicitors, so the data agreement, the legal aid account number and the
  site's statements about supervision by "your firm" do not fit them as written.
